# USS John C. Stennis showcase reference notes

Research date: 2026-11-09. Purpose: original artistic CAD scene, not a mechanically accurate carrier or operating procedure.

## Recommended setting

Use USS John C. Stennis (CVN-74), Carrier Air Wing 9, circa 2009–2010, with a VFA-146 Blue Diamonds F/A-18C Legacy Hornet. A Navy photograph dated 28 March 2009 explicitly identifies this combination and shows aircraft modex 301 in a launch view. Source S1. A second official Navy/DVIDS photograph from RIMPAC 2010 corroborates Blue Diamonds F/A-18C on Stennis, source S2.

A catapult-one, forward/starboard deck composition offers the clearest recognition: large white 74 deck number toward the bow, island aft and to starboard, grey non-skid deck, launch track under the nose gear, raised JBD behind the aircraft. Keep canopy shut and wing panels spread in the launch preset. The open canopy and folded wings should be separate inspection/parked presets. Do not put wheel chocks or tie-down chains on the launch-ready aircraft.

## Scale and geometry

The Navy's Air Forces Pacific fact page lists Nimitz-class overall length 1,092 ft / 332.85 m, hull beam 134 ft / 40.84 m, flight-deck width 252 ft / 76.8 m, and includes Stennis in the class. Treat these as reliable overall scale anchors, not exact surveyed offsets for Stennis. Some secondary sources say 257 ft maximum deck width, which may reflect ship-specific extremities or differing conventions; retain 76.8 m for the main deck and do not claim millimetre accuracy. Source S3.

The 2011 Stennis Public Affairs article describes a roughly 4.5-acre flight deck and confirms its air wing included F/A-18C and F/A-18E/F aircraft. Source S4.

For recognizable proportions:

- The main flight deck is a long, asymmetric polygon. Its port angled-landing area protrudes well beyond the hull amidships. The bow is broad and chamfered, not pointed like the hull.
- Two bow catapult tracks run forward, and two waist catapults are aft/port. Use four visible track systems if including the full ship.
- The landing strip runs diagonally across the port side toward the forward deck; use white edge lines and a dashed/segmented centreline. It is not parallel to the ship centreline.
- The island is on the starboard side, behind the bow launch area, with layered bridge/PriFly window bands, cantilevered platforms, rounded corners, railings, external ladders, antennas, mast and radar silhouettes.
- Aircraft elevators and their seam lines interrupt the deck edges. Their exact historical outline/positions should be traced from the chosen overhead Navy photo rather than inferred from a generic rectangle.
- Low sponsons, catwalks and angled safety nets sit below or outside the deck edge. Add these for low camera angles.

For visual detail without pretending to have engineering drawings, a modelling estimate for a bow-catapult JBD is about 12–15 m wide and 4–5 m front-to-back when flat. Its transverse hinge, broad shallow rectangular panel array, deep underside ribs and lifting struts matter more visually than a precise unsupported dimension. Raised angle around 45–55 degrees is an artistic pose estimate. Any such estimated dimensions must remain labelled as inferred.

## High-value close-up details

1. Catapult track: long dark slot bordered by worn grey steel strips; separate shallow rectangular shuttle under the lowered launch-bar visual. Add bolt seams and slightly varied steel patches, not tall rail tracks.
2. JBD: articulated rectangular bank behind the aircraft; darker scorched working face and strongly ribbed underside. Add thin panel seams, edge fasteners, exposed supporting arms and a shallow recessed deck pit. S5 is a close Navy maintenance view that clearly shows underside ribs, supporting structure, deck recess and wear.
3. Deck surface: very rough charcoal/blue-grey non-skid, patchy grey wear, lighter scuffs, rubber/soot traces concentrated near working areas, regular recessed circular tie-down fittings. Avoid exaggerated rust over the entire active deck.
4. Tie-down fittings: dark circular recesses with small internal cross/star features, shallow bevel and worn metallic rim; the main grid should read as inset details, not raised rivets.
5. Paint: off-white deck numbers and foul/landing lines, yellow taxi/working-area markings where supported by the chosen photo, and red/white danger boundaries near JBD areas. Keep markings thin and slightly weathered.
6. Island: large 74, grey bulkheads, dark green/blue reflective windows, platform undersides, railings, antenna arrays, radar frames, cable runs, navigation lights, life-raft containers and deck-edge equipment.
7. Human scale: optional yellow-shirt shooter/director and green-shirt launch crew in poses matching Navy photographs, with cranial helmets, hearing protection and flotation vests. Avoid scattering figures into unsafe aircraft paths for visual filler. Source S6 shows the look of final-checker crew alongside a Hornet.

## Materials

Use the closest existing Solidcraft material, then adjust visual roughness/colour where supported: rough painted steel for deck, pale navy-grey paint over steel for hull/island, weathered dark steel for JBD, brushed steel for catapult metal, dark glass for bridge windows, rubber for hoses and fenders, off-white painted metal for railings/life-raft canisters, aluminium for light housings. These are visual approximations; precise naval coating formulas are not established by these sources.

Keep water/sky and scene lights separate from ship geometry. For a future showcase, low forward three-quarter, cockpit close-up, landing-gear/catapult close-up, and high port three-quarter cameras reveal different aspects. A full-ship distant establishing view should not be expected to expose aircraft microdetail.

## Provenance and uncertainty

Build original geometry from public visual references. Do not extract/repackage DCS assets. The Navy photos have explicit public-domain notices but still carry DVIDS use terms and should retain photographer, service, VIRIN and source URL in the project credits. Do not imply Navy/DCS endorsement. Some page metadata dates conflict with photo captions (S7 EXIF date); use the dated caption and note the inconsistency if chronology matters.

Only overall scale and broad historical match are verified. Individual deck markings, exact component dimensions, radar fit and microdetail positions require tracing a consistent period photo. Distinguish detailed appearance from an engineering replica.
