# References, attribution and reconstruction notes

**Project:** F/A-18C NG301 / USS John C. Stennis (CVN-74) visual showcase  
**Research date:** 9 November 2026  
**Historical target:** VFA-146 Blue Diamonds, Carrier Air Wing 9, circa 2009

This project contains original procedural geometry, geometric lettering, display graphics and artistic material settings. Public sources informed the proportions, visible arrangement and historical setting. No DCS game mesh, simulator texture, third-party cockpit CAD, scanned airframe or downloaded model is incorporated.

## Historical and dimensional basis

The strongest aircraft/ship reference is the U.S. Navy photograph of **28 March 2009**, VIRIN **090328-N-6538W-004**: a VFA-146 F/A-18C launching from Stennis, with **301** visible on the aircraft. The model adopts that broad identity and low-visibility appearance. Its small markings and fittings are independently drawn interpretations; it is not an authenticated bureau-number reconstruction.

Nominal aircraft anchors are approximately **17.07 m length**, **11.43 m bare-wing span**, **12.31 m span over wingtip stores**, and **4.66 m height**. The landing-gear layout uses **3.11 m main-wheel track** and **5.42 m wheelbase** as consistency checks. The NASA three-view drawing is a silhouette reference for the Legacy Hornet; equipment on an operational C-model is cross-checked against Navy photographs and the DCS C-model guide. NAVAIR's public specification page contains inconsistent metric conversions: its 40 ft 5 in span is approximately 12.32 m, not 13.5 m.

The carrier envelope uses published **Nimitz-class** dimensions: **332.85 m overall length**, **40.84 m hull beam**, and **76.8 m flight-deck width**. These are class-level scale anchors. The detailed deck outline, island, catwalks, radar structures, elevators, fittings and markings are an artistic reconstruction, not surveyed Stennis engineering geometry.

## What is original

The source generators create the fuselage sections, wing and tail surfaces, canopy shells, cockpit components, pilot, landing gear, external stores, deck, island, JBD and scenery. Text is generated from a compact original geometric alphabet. Screen symbols, panel seams, fastener patterns, minor decals and material variation are also authored for this project. No reference image is used as a surface texture.

The U.S. Navy, NASA, Boeing, Martin-Baker, Eagle Dynamics and the referenced photographers are credited as reference sources, not project sponsors. The model and its documentation do not imply their endorsement. A future publication that separately incorporates the original photographs should retain the original credits and observe the linked source terms.

## Motion and material estimates

Joint axes, pivots, travel limits, contact clearances, small dimensions and actuator behavior are authored **visual-animation estimates**. They are not certified kinematics. Controls can be posed independently, so users should preserve a coherent scene configuration: launch views use a closed canopy, spread wings, lowered landing gear, stowed hook, closed speedbrake and engaged launch-bar appearance. Open-cockpit and folded-wing demonstrations are separate presentation states.

The cockpit includes independent short-travel joints for all 60 represented DDI/MPCD bezel buttons, the 12-key UFC keypad, its option/function buttons, warning buttons and the represented circuit breakers. Rotary knobs, large and small toggle switches, stick/throttle controls, pedal movements and prominent panel handles are also separately poseable. These are presentation controls: their motion does not operate instruments, aircraft systems or ordnance. Small-control travel is estimated; multi-direction hats are simplified to one illustrated rocking axis. The seated pilot is a static figure that follows the seat, with optional visibility for cockpit inspection. Seat webbing and the decorative ejection-handle loop are not mechanically simulated.

The public DCS guide informs the HALF-flap appearance: on the ground it depicts approximately 12-degree leading-edge flap deflection and 30-degree trailing-edge/aileron droop. Those reference values do not validate every moving surface or linkage in this project.

Materials use the nearest configured Solidcraft catalog family plus artist-selected visual properties. Gray exterior paint is rendered as a rough dielectric finish even when the substrate is metal. Bare exposed metals receive metallic settings; glass, fabric and dark polymer surfaces are separated. **Catalog fallbacks remain approximations**: the `composite` alias currently uses an Aluminum physical family for its painted visual surface; radome and tire aliases use ABS Plastic as generic nonmetal placeholders. Their appearance does not establish the real component's material, density or engineering properties. Colors and roughness are not spectrophotometer measurements, and rendered TPS grays are visual approximations.

The model is intended for Solidcraft showcase rendering, close-up visual exploration and articulation demonstrations. It is not a flight-training model, maintenance reference, naval-operating procedure, manufacturing design or structural analysis asset.
